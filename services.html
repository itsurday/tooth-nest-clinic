Check-up & cleaning

A regular check-up finds small problems before they become painful or expensive. Cleaning (scaling and polishing) removes the hard deposits that brushing cannot, and helps keep your gums healthy.

  • Every 6 months
  • 30–45 minutes
  • One visit

What happens

  • We ask about any pain, sensitivity or changes you have noticed.
  • Dr. Ramya checks every tooth, your gums, bite and the soft tissues of the mouth (including a screening for signs of oral cancer).
  • An X-ray is taken only if it is needed.
  • Scaling and polishing, then simple tips for brushing and flossing.

Afterwards

Gums may feel slightly tender for a day. Smokers and people who chew tobacco or paan may need cleaning more often.

Tooth-coloured fillings

Fillings repair teeth damaged by decay or small chips and cracks. We use tooth-coloured composite material that is shaded to match your teeth, so the repair is hard to see.

  • One visit
  • 30–60 minutes per tooth

What happens

  • The area is numbed if needed, so you feel pressure but no pain.
  • Decay is removed and the tooth is cleaned.
  • The filling is placed in layers, hardened with a blue light, then shaped and polished.

Afterwards

Numbness wears off in 2–3 hours; avoid hot drinks and chewing on that side until it does. Mild sensitivity for a few days is normal.

Root canal treatment

When decay or a crack reaches the nerve inside a tooth, it can cause strong pain or an abscess. Root canal treatment removes the infected nerve, cleans the inside of the tooth and seals it, so you can keep your own tooth.

With modern numbing, most patients say it feels much like having a filling.

  • 1–3 visits
  • 45–90 minutes each
  • Crown usually advised

What happens

  • An X-ray shows the shape of the roots and the infection.
  • The tooth is numbed and kept dry with a thin rubber sheet.
  • The canals are cleaned, shaped and filled, then the tooth is sealed.
  • A crown is often placed afterwards, because a root-treated tooth can become brittle.

Afterwards

The tooth may be tender for a few days; usual pain relief helps. Avoid biting hard on it until it is fully restored.

Crowns & bridges

A crown is a cap that covers and protects a weak, broken or root-treated tooth. A bridge replaces one or more missing teeth by fixing a false tooth to the teeth on either side of the gap.

  • 2 visits
  • About 1 week apart

What happens

  • First visit: the tooth is shaped, a mould or scan is taken and a temporary crown is fitted.
  • The crown or bridge is made in a dental laboratory in a shade chosen with you.
  • Second visit: it is checked for fit and bite, then fixed in place.

Afterwards

Brush and floss as usual; clean under a bridge with a floss threader or interdental brush. Avoid biting very hard objects.

Dental implants

An implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone that acts like a new tooth root. A crown is fixed on top. Implants do not rely on neighbouring teeth and, with good care, can last for many years.

  • Consultation + X-ray or 3D scan
  • 3–6 months healing
  • Staged payments

What happens

  • Consultation: we check your gums, bone and general health (for example diabetes or smoking) to see if implants suit you.
  • The implant is placed under local anaesthetic.
  • It heals and joins the bone over a few months; a temporary tooth can be worn meanwhile.
  • The final crown is made and fitted.

Afterwards

Expect some swelling for 2–3 days. Eat soft food, do not smoke, and keep the area clean as advised. Regular check-ups keep the implant healthy.

Dentures

Dentures are removable replacements for missing teeth: a partial denture for a few teeth, or a complete denture for a full arch. They help you chew, speak clearly and support the face.

  • 4–5 visits
  • Over 2–4 weeks

What happens

  • Moulds and measurements of your mouth and bite.
  • A trial fitting to check the look, shade and comfort.
  • Final fitting and adjustments, with follow-up visits for sore spots.

Afterwards

It takes a few weeks to get used to new dentures. Remove and clean them every day, keep them in water at night, and come back if anything rubs.

Cosmetic dentistry

Dr. Ramya is a cosmetic dental surgeon. Together you can plan changes to the colour, shape and line of your smile that still look natural.

  • Teeth whitening: in the clinic or with take-home trays. Teeth may feel sensitive for a few days. Whitening does not change the colour of existing fillings or crowns.
  • Veneers: thin porcelain or composite shells that cover the front of teeth to fix chips, gaps, stains or uneven shapes. Usually 2–3 visits.
  • Smile design: a combination of treatments, planned and previewed with you first.

What happens

  • A consultation about what you would like to change, with photos of your smile.
  • A check that teeth and gums are healthy first.
  • A written plan with options and costs.

Afterwards

Limit tea, coffee, tobacco and coloured foods after whitening. Veneers need the same care as natural teeth.

Braces & clear aligners

Orthodontic treatment straightens crooked or crowded teeth and corrects the bite. Options include metal braces, tooth-coloured ceramic braces and clear removable aligners.

  • 6–24 months
  • Check-ups every 4–6 weeks
  • Retainers afterwards

What happens

  • Assessment with photos, moulds or scans and X-rays.
  • A treatment plan showing the expected result and time.
  • Braces fitted or aligners given, with regular adjustment visits.
  • Retainers keep the teeth in their new place.

Afterwards

Teeth feel sore for a few days after each adjustment. Avoid hard and sticky foods with braces, and clean carefully around them.

Gum treatment

Bleeding, swollen or receding gums and bad breath are early signs of gum disease. Treated early, it can be stopped; left alone, it can loosen teeth.

  • 1–4 visits
  • Regular maintenance

What happens

  • Measuring the gum pockets around each tooth.
  • Deep cleaning below the gum line (root planing), under local anaesthetic if needed.
  • Review visits to check healing, and maintenance cleaning.

Afterwards

Brush twice a day, clean between teeth daily, and stop smoking or tobacco use if you can; it makes a big difference to gum health.

Children's dental care

A child's first dental visit should be around their first birthday or when the first tooth appears. Early, friendly visits help children grow up without fear of the dentist.

  • Short, friendly visits
  • Every 6 months

What we offer

  • Check-ups and gentle cleaning.
  • Fluoride application and fissure sealants to protect against decay.
  • Fillings for milk teeth and advice on thumb-sucking, diet and brushing.

Tips for parents

Brush twice daily with a rice-grain smear of fluoride toothpaste for under-3s and a pea-sized amount from age 3. Avoid sweet drinks in bottles at night.

Extractions & wisdom teeth

We always try to save a tooth, but sometimes removal is the healthiest choice: a badly broken or infected tooth, a wisdom tooth that keeps causing pain, or before braces.

  • One visit
  • Local anaesthetic

What happens

  • An X-ray to plan the extraction, and questions about your health and medicines (especially blood thinners).
  • The area is numbed fully before we begin.
  • Wisdom teeth may need a small surgical procedure.

Afterwards

Bite on the gauze for 30–45 minutes. For 24 hours, do not rinse, spit, use a straw, smoke or eat hot food. Then rinse gently with warm salt water. Call us if bleeding does not stop. Full aftercare guide.
