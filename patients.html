Your first visit

Your first appointment usually takes 30–45 minutes. Dr. Ramya will ask about your health and any worries, examine your teeth and gums, and talk you through what she finds and your options.

Please bring

  • A list of the medicines you take (or the strips/bottles).
  • Any recent dental X-rays or reports.
  • Details of health conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, heart problems, pregnancy or allergies.
  • A parent or guardian for children under 18.

When you arrive

  • Please come 10 minutes early to fill in a short health form.
  • Leave your footwear outside the door and wear a face mask in the waiting area.
  • We greet with a namaskar rather than a handshake, to keep hands clean.

Fees & payment

Every mouth is different, so we give you a written treatment plan and cost estimate after your examination, before any treatment starts. If there is more than one way to treat a problem, we explain the cost of each.

Please ask us for the current consultation fee when you book.

Paying in stages

Longer treatments such as implants, braces, or a root canal followed by a crown can be paid in stages as the treatment goes on.

Ways to pay

  • UPI (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm and others)
  • Debit and credit cards
  • Cash

You get a receipt for every payment. Ask us for an itemised bill if you plan to claim from health insurance or your employer.

Aftercare guides

Simple steps for a smooth recovery. If something does not feel right, call us.

After a filling
  • Wait until the numbness wears off (2–3 hours) before eating, so you do not bite your cheek or tongue.
  • Mild sensitivity to cold for a few days is normal.
  • If your bite feels high or uneven after a day, come back for a quick adjustment.
After a tooth extraction
  • Bite firmly on the gauze for 30–45 minutes.
  • For the first 24 hours: do not rinse, spit, use a straw, smoke, chew tobacco or drink alcohol, and avoid hot food and drinks.
  • Eat soft, cool food and chew on the other side.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals.
  • Some swelling is normal; a cold pack on the cheek helps on the first day. Take pain relief as advised.
  • If bleeding does not stop, bite on a clean folded gauze or a damp tea bag for 30 minutes and call us.
After root canal treatment
  • The tooth may be tender for a few days. Usual pain relief helps.
  • Avoid chewing hard food on that side until the tooth has its final filling or crown.
  • Keep the follow-up appointment for the crown; a root-treated tooth can crack without one.
After teeth whitening
  • For 48 hours, avoid tea, coffee, colas, turmeric-rich curries, beetroot and tobacco.
  • Sensitivity is common for a few days; a sensitivity toothpaste helps.
With new braces
  • Teeth feel sore for 3–5 days after fitting and adjustments; soft food helps.
  • Avoid hard and sticky foods (nuts, hard sweets, chewing gum, corn on the cob).
  • Brush carefully around every bracket and use an interdental brush.
  • If a wire pokes, cover it with orthodontic wax and call us.
With new dentures
  • Start with soft food cut into small pieces, and chew on both sides.
  • Take dentures out at night, clean them with a soft brush and keep them in water.
  • Sore spots are common at first; come back and we will adjust them.

Dental emergency: what to do now

Go to a hospital emergency department straight away if swelling spreads to your eye or neck, or makes it hard to breathe or swallow, or after a serious injury to the face or head.

For everything else, call us on 97047 37577 or WhatsApp us. Until you reach us:

A tooth has been knocked out
  1. Pick it up by the white crown, not the root.
  2. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly in milk or clean water. Do not scrub it.
  3. If you can, push it gently back into its socket and bite on a clean cloth.
  4. If not, keep it in a cup of milk (or inside the cheek, for adults).
  5. Come to us at once: the best chance of saving it is within 30–60 minutes.

Milk teeth in young children should not be put back in. Bring the child to us.

A broken or chipped tooth
  • Rinse with warm water and keep any broken pieces in milk.
  • Use a cold pack on the cheek for swelling.
  • Avoid chewing on that side and call us for an appointment.
Severe toothache
  • Rinse with warm salt water and gently floss to remove trapped food.
  • Take usual pain relief as directed on the pack. Do not put a tablet on the gum; it burns.
  • Call us. Pain that wakes you at night, or swelling, needs to be seen soon.
Bleeding after an extraction
  • Bite firmly on a clean folded gauze or a damp tea bag for 30 minutes without checking.
  • Sit upright and do not rinse.
  • If it is still bleeding heavily, call us, or go to a hospital if we are closed.
A lost crown or filling
  • Keep the crown safe and bring it with you.
  • Avoid very hot, cold or sweet food on that side, and call us for an appointment.

Nervous about the dentist?

You are not alone, and you do not need to apologise. Tell us when you book, and we will:

  • Start with a chat, with no treatment unless you are ready.
  • Explain each step before we do it, and agree a "stop" signal.
  • Use numbing gel before injections and take breaks whenever you want.
  • Book a quieter time of day if that helps.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I see the dentist?

Every 6 months for most people. If you have gum disease, smoke or chew tobacco, or have many fillings, we may suggest every 3–4 months.

Do you see children?

Yes. Bring your child for a first visit around their first birthday or when the first tooth appears. Early, friendly visits prevent fear later.

Is a dental X-ray safe?

Dental X-rays use a very small dose of radiation. We take them only when they help with a diagnosis. Please tell us if you are or might be pregnant.

Can I have dental treatment during pregnancy?

Yes. Check-ups and cleaning are safe and important, because gums can bleed more in pregnancy. Most routine treatment is best done in the middle three months. Tell us how many weeks pregnant you are.

I have diabetes or take blood thinners. Is that a problem?

No, but we need to know. Bring your medicine list. Do not stop any medicine on your own; we will plan treatment safely and talk to your doctor if needed.

Does root canal treatment hurt?

With good local anaesthetic, most people feel little more than during a filling. It relieves the pain of an infected tooth rather than causing it.

How long does teeth whitening last?

Usually one to two years, depending on tea, coffee and tobacco use. Touch-ups with take-home trays can keep the result fresh.

Do I need an appointment?

Booking is best so we can give you enough time. In pain? Call us and we will see you as soon as we can.

Changing or cancelling an appointment

Plans change. Please call or WhatsApp us at least 24 hours before your appointment so we can offer the time to someone in pain. If you are running late, let us know and we will do our best to fit you in.
